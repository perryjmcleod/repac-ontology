# REPAC vNext — Stress Test
## Digital Intake Rollout Failure Scenario

## Objective

Validate the semantic integrity of REPAC vNext by applying all nine supersets to a single real-world failure scenario.

This stress test evaluates whether each superset can be applied independently without semantic overlap, collapse, or ambiguity.

## Constraints

- No conceptual expansion
- No ontology drift
- No structural modification
- Use only normalized vNext definitions
- Enforce authoritative cross-superset boundary rules

## Scenario Description

A public-sector department launches a new digital intake form intended to replace email-based service requests.

The rollout fails within three weeks.

Front-line staff continue using the old email process. Managers believe the rollout was mandatory, but many staff treat it as optional. The new form captures incomplete information, which forces rework downstream. Some teams create local workarounds. Other teams wait for direction that never comes. Status meetings occur regularly, but cross-team coordination remains weak. Senior leadership believes the implementation is under control because reporting remains formally positive. Complaints increase from both internal users and citizens.

## Superset Classification

### Source

Source identifies the provenance or derivational basis of the material used in analysis.

Observed sources include:

- front-line staff reports  
- intake form data  
- email request archives  
- manager status updates  
- citizen complaints  
- downstream rework records  
- rollout documentation  
- observed workaround behaviour  

These elements represent the origin of evidence and analytical input.

### Scope

Scope defines the analytical frame within which examination occurs.

Relevant scope configurations include:

- internal operations only versus internal and citizen-facing impact  
- department-wide versus branch-level analysis  
- rollout week one versus full three-week interval  
- executive perspective versus front-line perspective  
- process-level versus case-level granularity  

These elements define how the analysis is bounded, oriented, and resolved.

### Purpose

Purpose defines the intended or declared reason for action.

Observed purposes include:

- replace email-based intake with a standardized digital process  
- improve consistency of request submission  
- reduce manual triage effort  
- increase visibility of incoming work  
- support downstream processing and reporting  
- position the initiative as modernization  

These elements represent the intended rationale for the rollout.

### Value

Value defines the significance or worth attributed to outcomes.

Relevant value considerations include:

- usefulness of the intake form  
- reduction in rework  
- service consistency  
- operational efficiency  
- trust in service delivery  
- completeness and reliability of submitted data  

These elements represent what makes outcomes matter.

### Imperative

Imperative defines the degree of obligation associated with action.

Observed imperative conditions include:

- perceived mandatory versus optional use of the new intake form  
- ambiguity regarding whether email intake was still permitted  
- inconsistent interpretation of rollout expectations across teams  

These elements represent obligation and requiredness.

### Governance

Governance defines the structural conditions that regulate authority, control, and accountability.

Relevant governance conditions include:

- authority to declare the rollout mandatory  
- allocation of accountability for adoption and quality  
- existence or absence of escalation mechanisms  
- definition of compliance expectations  
- oversight structures and reporting interpretation  
- legitimacy of local workaround decisions  

These elements define who could decide, enforce, and be held accountable.

### Work

Work defines the execution of actions undertaken to produce outcomes.

Observed work includes:

- submission of intake forms  
- continued use of email intake  
- manual correction of incomplete requests  
- downstream triage and routing  
- creation of local workaround processes  
- production of status reports  
- handling of service requests  

These elements represent actual activity and execution.

### Communication

Communication defines the transmission or exchange of information.

Observed communication includes:

- rollout announcements  
- training sessions  
- manager instructions  
- status meetings  
- reporting outputs  
- clarification exchanges  
- complaint submissions  

These elements represent information exchange.

### Collaboration

Collaboration defines the coordinated or emergent alignment of actors in shared activity.

Observed collaboration conditions include:

- limited alignment between front-line teams and management  
- inconsistent coordination across teams  
- fragmented response to incomplete intake data  
- localized adaptation without cross-team integration  

These elements represent the degree of alignment in action.

## Cross-Superset Observations

The failure does not originate from a single superset. It emerges from misalignment across multiple supersets:

- Purpose is declared but not operationalized  
- Value is not realized by front-line actors  
- Imperative is ambiguous, leading to inconsistent compliance  
- Governance does not clearly enforce or allocate accountability  
- Work adapts through local workaround behaviour  
- Communication is active but insufficient to produce alignment  
- Collaboration remains weak  
- Source signals exist but are not fully integrated  
- Scope may be constrained to formal reporting perspectives  

## Boundary Validation

This scenario confirms that each superset remains analytically distinct:

- Source and Scope remain separable (provenance vs framing)  
- Purpose and Value remain separable (intent vs significance)  
- Governance and Work remain separable (structure vs execution)  
- Communication and Collaboration remain separable (exchange vs alignment)  

No classification requires structural adjustment or conceptual expansion.

## Result

PASS

The REPAC vNext ontology maintains semantic integrity under applied conditions.

All nine supersets can be used concurrently without overlap or ambiguity when definitions are applied correctly.

## Residual Risk

The following boundaries remain most susceptible to misinterpretation in practice:

- Purpose ↔ Value  
- Imperative ↔ Governance  
- Governance ↔ Work  

These require continued discipline in language and application.

## Conclusion

The REPAC vNext model demonstrates strong semantic stability under real-world analytical pressure. The separation of supersets enables multi-dimensional diagnosis of complex organizational outcomes without collapsing domains into a single explanatory factor.

This stress test confirms that REPAC functions as a structured analytical system rather than a loosely interpreted framework.
