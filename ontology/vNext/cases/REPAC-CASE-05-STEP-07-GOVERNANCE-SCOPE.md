# REPAC Case 05 — Step 07: Governance and Scope

## Purpose of This Step
This step documents governance and scope conditions as they existed and were enacted in Case 05. Observations are descriptive and non-prescriptive.

## Governance Observations

### Formal Governance Signals
- Governance artifacts existed to define acceptable processes, roles, and decision authority.
- These artifacts assumed stable conditions and predictable execution paths.

### Enforcement and Oversight
- Oversight was uneven and often reactive rather than continuous.
- Compliance expectations were present, but enforcement varied with urgency and perceived risk.

### Exception Handling
- Exceptions were handled informally and inconsistently.
- Repeated exceptions became normalized through practice rather than governed explicitly.

## Scope Observations

### Defined Scope
- Scope definitions emphasized task ownership and functional boundaries.
- Dependencies and handoffs were under-specified.

### Enacted Scope
- Participants expanded scope pragmatically to resolve blockers and complete outcomes.
- Scope contraction occurred where activities were judged non-essential under pressure.

### Scope Implications
- Misalignment between defined and enacted scope increased reliance on individual judgment.
- Scope discipline functioned more as a reference than an enforceable operational boundary.
