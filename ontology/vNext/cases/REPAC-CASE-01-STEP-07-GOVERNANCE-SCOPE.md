# REPAC Case 01 — Step 7
## Governance–Scope Cross-Domain Constraint Mapping

## Case Reference
- Case ID: REPAC-CASE-01
- Case Title: Promotion Without Posting
- Framework Version: REPAC vNext
- Analysis Step: Step 7 — Cross-Domain Constraint Analysis
- Domains in Focus: Governance ↔ Scope
- Status: Active — Analytical Structuring Phase

## Purpose of This Artifact
This document records how Governance constraints shape, narrow, or override Scope commitments, and how Scope decisions in turn place pressure on Governance structures.

No causal attribution, fault, or diagnosis is made in this step.
The goal is to surface constraint interactions explicitly.

## Governance → Scope Constraint Pathways

### G→S.1 Posting Requirements as Scope-Defining Constraints
- Does governance require open posting for promotions?
- If so, posting requirements implicitly define:
  - eligible candidate population
  - decision timeline
  - evidence requirements
- Bypassing posting may implicitly narrow scope to pre-selected candidates.

### G→S.2 Exception Authority and Scope Reduction
- If exception authority exists:
  - who may invoke it?
  - under what conditions?
- Invocation of exception authority may:
  - legitimize reduced scope
  - compress evaluation criteria
  - eliminate comparative assessment

### G→S.3 Documentation and Auditability
- Governance requirements for documentation may:
  - expand scope (by requiring alternatives to be recorded)
  - or contract scope (by limiting justification to minimum compliance)

## Scope → Governance Stressors

### S→G.1 Time or Urgency Constraints
- Claims of urgency may be used to justify:
  - suspension of standard governance processes
  - reinterpretation of posting norms
- Question: does governance recognize urgency as a valid override?

### S→G.2 Implicit Exclusion Effects
- Narrowing scope (intentionally or implicitly) may:
  - create perceived governance violations
  - trigger legitimacy or trust concerns
- Governance may lack mechanisms to detect or address implicit exclusions.

### S→G.3 Scope Creep in Justification
- Post-hoc justifications may expand governance interpretation:
  - “This was an exception”
  - “This role was unique”
- Governance stability depends on whether such justifications are bounded.

## Constraint Interaction Summary (Non-Evaluative)

| Interaction | Observation |
|---|---|
| Governance defines scope | Posting rules and exception policies implicitly bound who may be considered |
| Scope pressures governance | Urgency and operational constraints may strain governance norms |
| Stability risk | Repeated scope-based exceptions may weaken governance legitimacy |
