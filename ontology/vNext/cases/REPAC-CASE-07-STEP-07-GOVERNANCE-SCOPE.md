# REPAC Case 07 — Step 07: Governance and Scope

## Purpose of This Step
This step documents governance and scope conditions as they existed and were enacted in Case 07. The objective is to describe how formal governance and scope constructs interacted with operational reality, without asserting prescriptive judgments or intervention guidance.

## Governance Observations

### Formal Governance Signals
- Governance artifacts existed to define acceptable processes, decision authority, and accountability.
- These artifacts assumed stable coordination patterns and predictable execution paths.

### Enforcement and Oversight
- Oversight was episodic and outcome-oriented rather than continuous.
- Deviations from formal expectations were identified primarily when downstream impacts surfaced.

### Exception Handling
- Exceptions were addressed informally, often without clear criteria or documentation.
- Recurrent exceptions became normalized through practice rather than governed explicitly.

### Accountability Dynamics
- Accountability tended to concentrate on individuals closest to execution.
- Responsibility for systemic effects was diffuse and weakly articulated.

## Scope Observations

### Defined Scope
- Scope definitions emphasized task ownership and functional boundaries.
- Cross-boundary dependencies were under-specified or assumed to be managed externally.

### Enacted Scope
- Participants expanded scope pragmatically to resolve coordination gaps.
- Scope contraction occurred where activities were perceived as non-essential to immediate outcomes.

### Scope Implications
- Divergence between defined and enacted scope increased reliance on tacit knowledge.
- Scope discipline functioned as a reference rather than an enforceable operational boundary.
