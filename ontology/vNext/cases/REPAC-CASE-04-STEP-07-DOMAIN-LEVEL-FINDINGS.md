# REPAC Case 04 — Step 07: Domain-Level Findings

## Purpose of This Step
This step records domain-specific observations without collapsing them into causal claims. Findings are descriptive and comparative, focused on mismatches between formal constructs and enacted reality.

## Findings by Domain

### Purpose
High performers demonstrated strong alignment with organizational outcomes such as service quality and delivery speed, even when bypassing formal processes. The shadow process was not purpose-deviant; it was purpose-compensatory.

### Imperative
Operational imperatives favored speed, reliability, and reduced friction. Formal processes introduced delays or overhead that conflicted with these imperatives, particularly under high workload or time pressure.

### Scope
The formal process defined scope narrowly and rigidly, while real work required adaptive boundary crossing. The shadow process expanded scope pragmatically to include activities necessary to complete outcomes end-to-end.

### Work
Work in the shadow process was streamlined and experience-driven. Steps perceived as redundant or low-value were omitted, while informal checks replaced formal controls.

### Governance
Governance mechanisms existed but were weakly enforced. Exception handling was informal and discretionary, allowing shadow practices to persist without explicit authorization.

### Communication
Knowledge of the shadow process circulated informally through peer networks rather than formal documentation or training. Communication emphasized “what works” over “what is approved.”

### Collaboration
Collaboration was selective and trust-based. High performers relied on known, reliable counterparts rather than formally assigned roles.

### Value
Short-term value outcomes improved measurably (speed, quality, customer satisfaction). Long-term risks, including auditability gaps, dependency on individuals, and scalability constraints, remained largely unexamined.
