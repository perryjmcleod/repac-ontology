# REPAC Case 06 — Step 07: Governance and Scope

## Purpose of This Step
This step characterizes governance and scope conditions as they existed and were enacted in Case 06. Observations are descriptive, not evaluative, and are intended to support later synthesis rather than prescribe remediation.

## Governance Observations

### Formal Governance Structures
- Governance artifacts existed to define roles, approvals, and acceptable processes.
- These artifacts assumed stable conditions and predictable execution paths.

### Enforcement and Oversight
- Oversight mechanisms were unevenly applied across time and teams.
- Compliance expectations were communicated, but enforcement varied with urgency and perceived risk.

### Exception Handling
- Exceptions were managed informally, often without documentation or escalation.
- Repeated exceptions became normalized rather than explicitly governed.

## Scope Observations

### Defined Scope
- Scope definitions emphasized task completion rather than end-to-end outcome realization.
- Dependencies and handoffs were under-specified.

### Enacted Scope
- Participants expanded scope pragmatically to resolve issues blocking completion.
- Scope contraction also occurred, with non-essential activities deferred or omitted.

### Scope Implications
- Misalignment between defined and enacted scope increased reliance on individual judgment.
- Scope discipline functioned more as a reference point than an operational constraint.
