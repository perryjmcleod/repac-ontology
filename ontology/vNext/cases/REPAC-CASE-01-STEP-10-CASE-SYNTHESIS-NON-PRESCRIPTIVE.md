## Case Summary (Descriptive)

A promotion decision was made without a formal posting or open competition.  
The decision was experienced by at least one affected party as procedurally irregular, raising questions related to governance, scope, communication, collaboration, value, and legitimacy.

The case is not defined by the outcome itself, but by the **interaction between decision process, boundary setting, meaning transfer, and perceived value effects**.

---

## Cross-Step Integration

### Governance and Scope
- Governance mechanisms implicitly define who is eligible to participate.
- Scope decisions, whether explicit or implicit, shape how governance is perceived.
- Exceptions, when not visibly bounded, increase interpretive ambiguity.

### Communication and Collaboration
- Communication timing and framing influence whether collaboration is possible.
- Collaboration absence shifts explanatory burden onto communication.
- Information transfer alone does not constitute coordinated participation.

### Value and Legitimacy
- Value outcomes and legitimacy perceptions are interdependent but non-identical.
- Governance compliance does not guarantee legitimacy.
- Legitimacy conditions affect how value outcomes are interpreted and sustained.

---

## Analytical Pattern (Non-Causal)

This case exhibits a recurring organizational pattern:

> Decisions that are procedurally bounded but weakly contextualized tend to shift explanatory weight onto governance legitimacy and relational trust, regardless of operational outcomes.

This pattern is **descriptive**, not predictive.

## What This Case *Does* Demonstrate

- The insufficiency of single-domain explanations
- The importance of explicit boundary signaling
- The analytical value of separating:
  - compliance from legitimacy
  - communication from collaboration
  - outcome from explanation
