## Purpose of This Artifact

This document records how **value impacts** (realized or anticipated) intersect with **governance legitimacy** in the case, without asserting causality, blame, or correctness.

This step:
- Does **not** evaluate merit or performance
- Does **not** adjudicate policy compliance
- Does **not** claim reputational harm or benefit

## Value Impact Surfaces

The following identify where value may be created, diminished, or redistributed as a result of the decision.

### V.1 Operational Value
- Continuity of operations
- Speed of role fulfillment
- Short-term efficiency gains or losses

### V.2 Human and Relational Value
- Perceived fairness among staff
- Morale and motivation
- Trust in managerial processes

### V.3 Capability and Development Value
- Signaling of advancement pathways
- Incentives for skill development
- Retention or disengagement risks

### V.4 Risk and Cost Value
- Risk exposure from process deviation
- Cost of remediation or turnover
- Future governance overhead

---

## Legitimacy Surfaces (Governance-Linked)

The following identify how governance legitimacy may be affected independently of policy compliance.

### L.1 Procedural Legitimacy
- Alignment between stated governance norms and observed actions
- Visibility of rule application or exception handling

### L.2 Explanatory Legitimacy
- Adequacy of reasons provided to affected parties
- Consistency of explanation across audiences

### L.3 Institutional Legitimacy
- Confidence in governance systems as stable and predictable
- Perception of decision-making impartiality

### L.4 Relational Legitimacy
- Acceptance of decisions by those excluded from participation
- Willingness to engage future processes in good faith

---

## Value ↔ Legitimacy Interaction Pathways

The following mappings surface how value and legitimacy may reinforce or undermine each other.

### V→L.1 Outcome Acceptance Pathway
- Positive or neutral value outcomes may:
  - Increase tolerance for governance exceptions
- Negative or ambiguous value outcomes may:
  - Heighten scrutiny of process legitimacy

### L→V.1 Trust-to-Value Conversion
- Strong legitimacy may:
  - Preserve morale despite contested outcomes
  - Enable value realization over time
- Weakened legitimacy may:
  - Erode realized value even if outcomes are operationally sound

### V↔L.2 Distribution Effects
- Value concentrated in one area (e.g., speed) may:
  - Reduce legitimacy if trade-offs are opaque
- Broadly distributed value may:
  - Reinforce legitimacy even under constraint
