## Purpose of This Artifact

This document records how **Communication practices enable, constrain, or substitute for Collaboration**, and how **Collaboration structures shape what communication can achieve** in the context of the case.

This step:
- Does **not** evaluate quality or effectiveness
- Does **not** assign fault or intent
- Does **not** assume communication or collaboration failure

The goal is to make **interaction effects explicit** so later analysis does not collapse meaning transfer into joint action, or vice versa.

## Communication → Collaboration Constraint Pathways

The following mappings surface how communication choices affect the possibility, timing, and shape of collaboration.

### C→Co.1 Timing of Communication and Opportunity for Collaboration
- Communication that occurs **after** a decision:
  - Limits opportunities for joint sensemaking
  - Converts collaboration into reaction or escalation
- Communication that occurs **before** a decision:
  - Enables input, challenge, or alignment
  - Allows collaboration to influence outcomes

### C→Co.2 Content Framing and Collaborative Space
- Communication framed as:
  - *Informational* (“This has been decided”)
  - *Procedural* (“This followed policy”)
  - *Exceptional* (“This was a special case”)
- Each framing constrains:
  - What questions are legitimate
  - Whether collaboration is invited or foreclosed

### C→Co.3 Channel Selection and Actor Inclusion
- Choice of communication channels (e.g., private vs public):
  - Determines who can participate
  - Signals whether collaboration is expected
- Limited-channel communication may implicitly exclude potential collaborators.

## Collaboration → Communication Stressors

The following mappings identify how collaboration structures (or their absence) place pressure on communication.

### Co→C.1 Absence of Pre-Decision Collaboration
- Where collaboration does not occur prior to decision:
  - Communication must carry explanatory burden
  - Justification may substitute for shared understanding
- This increases sensitivity to wording, tone, and completeness.

### Co→C.2 Role Ambiguity and Message Interpretation
- Unclear collaborative roles (e.g., HR vs management vs peers):
  - Create ambiguity about message authority
  - Increase risk of misinterpretation
- Communication may be received as discretionary even if intended as procedural.

### Co→C.3 Feedback and Escalation Pathways
- Availability (or lack) of collaborative feedback mechanisms:
  - Shapes how communication is responded to
  - Determines whether concerns surface informally or escalate formally
- Communication without a collaboration pathway may amplify frustration.

## Interaction Patterns Observed (Non-Evaluative)

| Interaction Pattern | Structural Implication |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| Late communication | Collaboration limited to post-decision response |
| Narrow audience | Collaboration constrained by actor inclusion |
| Justificatory framing | Collaboration shifted from co-creation to acceptance |
| Missing feedback loop | Communication becomes terminal, not dialogic |
