# Scope Superset  
## Layer-1 Collision Audit  
### Targets: Purpose, Imperative, Work, Governance  
### Status: Draft (Pre-Lock)

---

## Audit Rule

A Layer-1 Scope subset is invalid if it:
- duplicates a delimitation already owned by another Superset,
- encodes prescriptive sequencing or control,
- reifies “context” as an entity,
- or functions as a proxy for Purpose, Imperative, Work, or Governance.

Scope bounds consideration. It does not justify, direct, execute, or enforce.

---

## 1. Inclusion Boundary

### Potential Collisions
- **Purpose:** inclusion framed as “what matters” (teleology) rather than what is bounded.
- **Imperative:** inclusion framed as “must include” due to obligation rather than delimitation.
- **Work:** inclusion framed as “tasks in scope” as a work breakdown or process model.
- **Governance:** inclusion framed as “policy coverage” or compliance domain rather than scope frame.

### Guardrails
- Inclusion is an **analytic boundary statement**, not a prioritization, requirement, plan, or control.
- State inclusion as **eligible-for-consideration**, not “important,” “required,” or “mandated.”

### Pass/Fail
- **PASS**, conditional on language discipline.

---

## 2. Exclusion Boundary

### Potential Collisions
- **Purpose:** exclusion justified as “not aligned” (purpose filtering) rather than frame delimitation.
- **Imperative:** exclusion justified as “not allowed” (normative prohibition) rather than out-of-frame.
- **Work:** exclusion treated as “out of backlog/sprint” (process framing).
- **Governance:** exclusion treated as “not permitted by policy” (governance control).

### Guardrails
- Exclusion is **out-of-frame**, not “forbidden,” “noncompliant,” or “deprioritized.”
- Exclusion may record governance-related reasons, but does not become governance.

### Pass/Fail
- **PASS**, conditional on not becoming a disguised rule system.

---

## 3. Granularity Level

### Potential Collisions
- **Purpose:** granularity selected to serve values/ends (teleological rationale) rather than resolution.
- **Imperative:** granularity treated as mandated method (“must be detailed”) rather than resolution choice.
- **Work:** granularity collapses into decomposition of work deliverables (WBS) instead of analytic resolution.
- **Governance:** granularity treated as audit compliance requirement rather than analytic resolution boundary.

### Guardrails
- Granularity defines **resolution of explanation**, not decomposition of execution.
- Avoid mapping granularity to specific methods, artefact types, or process stages.

### Pass/Fail
- **PASS**, conditional on not drifting into work decomposition.

---

## 4. Actor Boundary

### Potential Collisions
- **Purpose:** actor boundary used to privilege stakeholders (“primary stakeholders”) as value logic.
- **Imperative:** actor boundary used to encode responsibility/accountability (RACI ownership).
- **Work:** actor boundary becomes role-task assignment or operational responsibility.
- **Governance:** actor boundary becomes authority model or governance hierarchy.

### Guardrails
- Actor Boundary defines **which actors are considered in-frame**, not who should do what.
- Responsibility, accountability, authority, and delegation remain outside Scope.

### Pass/Fail
- **PASS**, with strict separation from accountability/authority semantics.

---

## 5. Artefact Boundary

### Potential Collisions
- **Purpose:** artefact boundary set by “what matters” rather than what is in-frame.
- **Imperative:** artefact boundary becomes compliance artefact list (“must produce X”).
- **Work:** artefact boundary becomes deliverable breakdown or project plan artefact list.
- **Governance:** artefact boundary becomes mandated documentation regime.

### Guardrails
- Artefact Boundary defines **objects considered**, not deliverables required or produced.
- Do not encode lifecycle state, ownership, or approval requirements.

### Pass/Fail
- **PASS**, conditional on non-prescriptive framing.

---

## 6. Constraint Boundary

### Potential Collisions
- **Purpose:** constraints confused with value trade-offs or ends-based justification.
- **Imperative:** constraints treated as obligations (“must”) rather than limiting conditions.
- **Work:** constraints treated as execution blockers in a workflow sense.
- **Governance:** constraints treated as rules/policies (governance) rather than limiting conditions.

### Guardrails
- Constraint Boundary captures **limiting conditions treated as delimiters** for analysis.
- Governance rules may be cited as a source of constraint, but the constraint boundary does not model governance itself.
- Constraints are not “context objects”; they are boundary conditions declared for the analytic frame.

### Pass/Fail
- **PASS**, with the highest risk of drift into Governance/Imperative.

---

## Summary Decision

All six candidates remain eligible at Layer-1 with strict language discipline.

Highest collision risk:
1. Constraint Boundary (Governance/Imperative drift)
2. Actor Boundary (Governance/Work drift)
3. Artefact Boundary (Work/Governance drift)
