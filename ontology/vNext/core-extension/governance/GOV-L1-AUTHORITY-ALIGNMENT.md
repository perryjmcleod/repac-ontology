# GOV-L1 — Authority Alignment (SC-1)

## Status
Draft — vNext core-extension governance clarification.

## Purpose
This artifact clarifies analytically distinct governance constructs that are frequently conflated in practice. The purpose is to enable disciplined diagnosis of governance failure modes without implying prescription.

## Scope
This clarification operates within Governance Layer 1 (L1). It does not introduce new Supersets. It distinguishes governance-relevant constructs so that they can be tested independently during analysis.

## Constructs

### Authority
A formal or recognized mandate to define rules, direction, or boundaries.

**Analytical test:** Who is formally empowered?

### Decision Rights
Explicit or implicit permission to make specific determinations within a defined boundary.

**Analytical test:** Who is allowed to decide?

### Control
Operational capacity to enforce, execute, or implement decisions, including the practical ability to cause actions to occur.

**Analytical test:** Who actually executes or enforces?

### Accountability
Assignment of consequence absorption for outcomes, including responsibility attribution when outcomes deviate.

**Analytical test:** Who absorbs impact when outcomes deviate?

## Alignment Rule (Analytical)
The following constructs are treated as analytically separable:

- Authority does not imply Control.
- Control does not imply Legitimacy.
- Decision Rights do not imply Authority.
- Accountability does not imply Decision Rights.

## Diagnostic Use
This clarification enables analysis of governance failure patterns such as:

- Authority exercised without Decision Rights being formally assigned.
- Decision Rights exercised without formal Authority.
- Decisions made without Control to implement or enforce.
- Control exercised without legitimacy or formal mandate.
- Accountability assigned without corresponding Decision Rights or Control.

## Non-Prescriptive Boundary
This artifact supports explanation and diagnosis only. It does not specify what governance *should* be; it specifies what governance-related constructs can be distinguished and tested during analysis.
