# IMPERATIVE — Definition

## Definition

Imperative defines the degree of obligation associated with action, expression, or compliance. It establishes whether something must be done or may be done, independent of what is being done or why it is pursued.

## Clarification

Imperative governs obligation. It expresses the force or requirement behind action, ranging from mandatory to discretionary.

Imperative does not define intent, value, execution, or authority. It defines whether action is required, expected, or optional.

## Boundary Statement

Imperative is distinct from Purpose, Governance, and Work.

Imperative defines obligation.  
Purpose defines intent.  
Governance defines authority and control.  
Work defines execution.

Imperative answers whether something must be done.  
Purpose answers why it is done.  
Governance determines who can decide and enforce.  
Work performs the action.

## Critical Note

Imperatives manifest through sources and are often expressed through communication, but they do not originate from execution or define governance structures themselves.

An imperative may exist without enforcement, and enforcement may exist without a clearly articulated imperative. These must remain analytically separate.

## Prohibited Constructions

The following constructions are not valid within the REPAC ontology:

- imperative equals purpose  
- imperative equals governance  
- imperative equals execution  
- obligation defined as authority  
- mandatory action treated as governance structure  
- discretionary action treated as absence of imperative
