# VNEXT-0007 — Pilot Evaluation Criteria

## Purpose
Define the criteria used to evaluate whether a pilot decomposition improves analytical clarity without introducing prescriptive or methodological drift.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Discriminative Power
Does the pilot allow analysts to distinguish meaningfully different Scope commitments across cases?

### 2. Superset Integrity
Do all constructs remain strictly dependent on their parent Superset without redefining it?

### 3. Non-Overlap
Do constructs avoid duplicating semantics from other Supersets (Purpose, Work, Governance, Source, etc.)?

### 4. Analyst Usability
Can a competent analyst apply the constructs without being given a procedure or sequence?

### 5. Reject Pressure
Does the model make it easy to reject tempting but invalid constructs (roles, steps, metrics)?

### 6. Stability
Do constructs remain interpretable across multiple analyses without contextual tailoring?

## Failure Conditions
A pilot fails if any construct:
- reads as “how to do the work,”
- implies better or worse performance,
- introduces temporal or maturity language,
- requires procedural explanation to apply.

## Outcome
Pilot success is defined by improved explanation, not adoption, execution, or optimization.
