# Governance — Core Claims (Pilot)

1. Governance models the allocation and exercise of decision rights, accountability, and control constraints that shape what actions are permissible, expected, or blocked.
2. Governance is necessary but not sufficient for organisational explanation; it constrains Work and conditions Value outcomes without determining them alone.
3. Governance claims must be anchored to evidence artifacts (e.g., policy instruments, delegations, approvals, audit trails, exception records), not post-hoc attribution.
4. Governance failure is analysable as mismatch among stated decision rights, enacted decision rights, and evidenced accountability or control.
5. Governance includes both formal structures (documented roles, committees, delegated authorities) and enacted structures (what is actually authorised, tolerated, or enforced).
6. Governance is expressed through controls, including gates, reviews, compliance checks, and monitoring signals; absence of enforceable controls is a governance condition, not merely a communication gap.
7. Governance interacts with Scope via boundary authority: who can set, revise, defer, or waive inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, and constraints.
8. A Scope deviation is governance-relevant when it reflects unauthorised boundary change, exception handling, or control bypass rather than a legitimate, evidenced adjustment.
9. Governance explanations must separate exception handling from non-compliance: an exception is only an exception when it is authorised, recorded, and discoverable under the governing ruleset.
10. Governance interacts with Work by authorising transformations, sequencing constraints, and permissible methods, while remaining analytically distinct from execution quality.
11. Governance interacts with Value by allocating accountability for outcomes and by defining what trade-offs are acceptable, reviewable, or prohibited.
12. “Weak governance” is an insufficient explanation unless it is decomposed into specific failure modes (e.g., ambiguous authority, conflicting decision rights, ineffective controls, non-discoverable exceptions, or shadow governance).
