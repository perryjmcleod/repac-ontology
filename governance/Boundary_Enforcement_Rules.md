# REPAC Ontology Boundary Enforcement Rules (v1.0)

This document defines the formal boundary enforcement rules governing the REPAC Ontology v1.0. These rules exist to preserve analytical clarity, prevent category collapse, and guarantee consistent application of the ontology across analyses, extensions, and scholarly use.

All rules in this document are binding for REPAC Ontology v1.0.

## Purpose of Boundary Enforcement

REPAC is a structural analytical ontology. Its explanatory power depends on strict separation between analytically necessary domains. Boundary enforcement prevents the collapse of distinct Supersets into narrative explanations, causal shortcuts, or methodological substitutions.

Boundary enforcement rules apply to:

- Ontology modeling and extension
- Analytical application and walkthroughs
- Academic interpretation and critique
- Tooling, visualization, and formal encoding

Violations of these rules invalidate REPAC-based analysis.

## Superset Non-Substitution Rule

No Superset may substitute for another Superset.

Analytical explanations that attribute outcomes primarily or exclusively to a single Superset violate this rule. Statements such as governance failure caused the result or communication breakdown explains the failure' are analytically invalid within REPAC.

Each Superset is necessary but not sufficient. Explanations must account for interactions across Supersets.

## Superset Boundary Integrity Rule

Each Superset represents a distinct analytical domain with non-overlapping intent.

Concepts assigned to one Superset must not be duplicated, reinterpreted, or absorbed into another Superset for explanatory convenience. Boundary crossing must be explicit and justified through defined relationships, not narrative implication.

## No Context Reification Rule

Context must not be introduced as a first-class construct.

Contextual explanations must be decomposed into explicit Superset-level constructs such as Scope conditions, Governance constraints, Work structure, or Communication coherence. Introducing context as an explanatory placeholder violates REPAC analytical discipline.

## Non-Causal Attribution Rule

REPAC does not permit linear causal attribution.

Superset relationships represent constraint interaction and influence, not event causation. Analytical accounts that imply temporal or causal chains, but are free of explicit Superset interaction analysis, are invalid.

## Work Activity Non-Primacy Rule

The presence or absence of Work activity must not be treated as primary evidence of success or failure.

Work is evaluated only after examining upstream Supersets, including Purpose, Scope, Governance, and Value. Treating execution quality as a root explanation violates REPAC analytical ordering.

## Value Independence Rule

Value must be analytically distinct from Work completion.

Completion of tasks, delivery of artifacts, or fulfillment of schedules does not imply Value realization. Value must be explicitly defined and evaluable independently of Work activity.

## Communication Non-Volume Rule

Communication success must not be inferred from communication volume.

High levels of messaging, meetings, or documentation do not indicate analytical alignment. Communication is evaluated based on shared interpretation among Supersets, not frequency or transparency alone.

## Collaboration Structural Rule

Collaboration must be evaluated structurally rather than socially.

Interpersonal cooperation, goodwill, or effort does not constitute sufficient Collaboration under REPAC. Collaboration requires defined roles, coordination mechanisms, and alignment with Governance constraints.

## Interaction Accumulation Rule

REPAC prohibits single-point explanations.

Analytical conclusions must demonstrate how insufficiencies accumulate and interact across multiple Supersets. Failure to show cross-Superset interaction constitutes incomplete analysis.

## Analytical Neutrality Rule

REPAC analysis must remain analytically neutral.

The ontology must not be used to justify predetermined conclusions, assign blame, or support prescriptive recommendations. REPAC explains structural conditions, not corrective actions.

## Version Lock Rule

These boundary enforcement rules apply exclusively to REPAC Ontology v1.0.

Any modification to these rules requires explicit governance approval and version increment. No retrospective reinterpretation of v1.0 is permitted.

## Relationship to Other Artifacts

These boundary enforcement rules govern:

- The Worked Failure Walkthrough
- Formal ontology artifacts in OWL and TTL
- Visual ontology diagrams
- Academic paper arguments and examples

All REPAC artifacts must remain consistent with these rules.
