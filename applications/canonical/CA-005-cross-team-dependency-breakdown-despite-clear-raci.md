# CA-005 — Cross-Team Dependency Breakdown Despite Clear RACI

**Status:** Canonical Application (Non-Normative)  
**Ontology Version:** REPAC v1.0 (Immutable)  
**Purpose:** Demonstration of analytical use only  
**Boundary Note:** This document does not introduce, modify, extend, or reinterpret REPAC ontology constructs. It applies REPAC v1.0 as published.

---

## 1. Case Claim (The Misdiagnosis)

The initiative stalled because teams **failed to follow the RACI**.

When handoffs broke down and deliverables slipped, leadership attributed the issue to role confusion or lack of discipline. The presence of a formally approved RACI matrix reinforced the belief that responsibility was already clear and that execution was at fault.

---

## 2. Observed Outcomes (Without Interpretation)

- A detailed RACI matrix was created and approved.
- Roles and accountabilities were communicated.
- Teams acknowledged their responsibilities.
- Dependencies between teams persisted.
- Deliverables were delayed or incomplete.
- Escalations increased despite role clarity.
- Teams expressed frustration with upstream and downstream partners.

Formally, responsibility was defined. Practically, coordination failed.

---

## 3. REPAC Domain Walkthrough

REPAC analysis examines each domain as **necessary but not sufficient**, avoiding reduction to role artifacts.

---

### 3.1 Purpose

- The RACI was introduced to improve coordination and accountability.
- Success was framed as clarity of ownership.
- No explicit purpose addressed interdependence management.

**Observation:** Purpose stabilized accountability, not coordination.

---

### 3.2 Source

- Role definitions were derived from organizational charts and past projects.
- Limited analysis of actual work coupling informed the matrix.
- Assumptions about handoff simplicity went untested.

**Observation:** Source inputs reflected structure more than interaction.

---

### 3.3 Imperative

- Teams were expected to adhere to assigned roles.
- Escalation was encouraged when boundaries were crossed.
- Collaboration outside defined responsibilities was discouraged.

**Observation:** Imperative constrained adaptive coordination.

---

### 3.4 Scope

- Scope focused on defining who does what.
- Timing, sequencing, and negotiation of dependencies were out of scope.
- Variability in capacity and priorities was excluded.

**Observation:** Scope omitted the mechanics of dependency resolution.

---

### 3.5 Work

- Teams executed tasks within their defined roles.
- Handoffs required negotiation beyond role definitions.
- Work stalled when dependencies conflicted with local priorities.

**Observation:** Work required coordination not specified by RACI.

---

### 3.6 Value

- Value was assumed to follow from role clarity.
- Delays and rework eroded expected benefits.
- Productivity losses were attributed to execution failure.

**Observation:** Value assumptions ignored dependency friction.

---

### 3.7 Governance

- Governance bodies approved the RACI.
- Escalations reinforced role boundaries.
- Structural clarity was treated as governance success.

**Observation:** Governance enforced role definition, not flow.

---

### 3.8 Communication

- Communication focused on reminding teams of their roles.
- Dependency conflicts surfaced as complaints.
- Sense-making about systemic coupling was limited.

**Observation:** Communication reiterated structure rather than resolving interaction.

---

### 3.9 Collaboration

- Collaboration was expected to occur within role constraints.
- Cross-team negotiation lacked formal support.
- Informal relationships became critical to progress.

**Observation:** Collaboration compensated for structural omission.

---

## 4. Why “Failure to Follow the RACI” Is an Attractive Explanation

Blaming non-adherence to RACI is appealing because it:

- Preserves confidence in governance artifacts
- Frames breakdowns as behavioral issues
- Suggests enforcement or clarification as remedies
- Avoids examining interdependence complexity

REPAC analysis shows that role clarity alone is not explanatory.

---

## 5. REPAC-Compliant Explanation

Dependency breakdown resulted from **cross-domain misalignment**.

Specifically:

- Purpose emphasized accountability over coordination.
- Source inputs abstracted roles from real work coupling.
- Imperative discouraged adaptive negotiation.
- Scope excluded dependency mechanics.
- Work required interaction beyond role definitions.
- Value assumptions ignored coordination cost.
- Governance enforced structure.
- Communication reinforced boundaries.
- Collaboration adapted informally.

The RACI did not fail.  
It **successfully clarified responsibility while leaving dependency unresolved**.

---

## 6. Boundary Reflection

REPAC does not recommend alternative role models or coordination tools.

This analysis does not propose:
- Revising the RACI
- Adding more roles
- Introducing new forums
- Implementing dependency management techniques

It demonstrates that artifacts clarifying responsibility cannot substitute for explicit analytical treatment of interdependence.

REPAC’s role is to expose this limitation without prescribing correction.
