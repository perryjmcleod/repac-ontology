# Worked Failure Walkthrough  
A REPAC Ontology Analytical Demonstration

## Purpose of the Walkthrough

This walkthrough demonstrates how the REPAC Ontology is applied as an analytical reasoning structure to examine organizational failure. The objective is not to diagnose a specific organization, recommend corrective actions, or prescribe best practices. The purpose is to show how analytical clarity develops when work outcomes are examined by explicit ontological domains rather than narrative explanation or post-hoc attribution.

This walkthrough serves as the canonical analytical example for REPAC Ontology v1.0.

## Scenario Overview

An organization initiates a cross-functional digital transformation initiative intended to modernize internal systems and optimize service delivery. The initiative is formally approved, funded, and staffed. Eighteen months later, the program was terminated after exceeding budget, missing key milestones, and failing to deliver usable outcomes.

Post-termination reviews produce conflicting explanations. Leadership cites execution failures. Delivery teams cite unclear priorities and governance. Stakeholders cite communication lapses. No shared explanation emerges.

This walkthrough applies REPAC to examine the failure without privileging any sole narrative.

## Analytical Entry Point

REPAC analysis does not begin with blame, root cause speculation, or sequence reconstruction. The analysis starts by examining whether each analytical domain required for successful work was sufficiently instantiated, aligned, and constrained.

The walkthrough proceeds domain by domain, illustrating analytical gaps and mismatches.

## Purpose

The stated purpose of the initiative emphasized modernization and capability but lacked explicit articulation of the intended value realization. Purpose statements were aspirational rather than operational. There was no common understanding of what success would mean concretely beyond general improvement.

Analytically, Purpose existed but was underspecified. It provided motivational framing but insufficient analytical constraint.

REPAC requires that Purpose be evaluated as an analytical constraint, not as an aspirational statement. The rule applied is that Purpose must be sufficiently specified to constrain Scope, Governance, and Value interpretation.

In the walkthrough, Purpose is examined for analytical sufficiency rather than rhetorical clarity. Although a purpose statement existed, it did not define evaluable outcomes or decision constraints. Under REPAC rules, an under-specified Purpose weakens downstream analytical coherence because it cannot function as a stabilizing reference for trade-offs or ranking.

The ontology does not assess whether the Purpose was inspiring or well-intended. It evaluates whether Purpose could be used analytically to regulate decisions across other Supersets. In this case, it could not.

## Scope

The initiative’s scope expanded repeatedly in response to stakeholder requests. Boundaries between core deliverables and optional enhancements were not enforced. Scope decisions were responsive rather than governed.

From a REPAC perspective, Scope lacked stabilization. The absence of enforced scope boundaries created analytical ambiguity regarding what work was in or out of bounds at any given time.

REPAC applies the rule that Scope must be explicitly bounded and governed. Scope is not treated as a passive description of work but as a deliberate analytical constraint that determines what work is legitimate at any given time.

In the walkthrough, Scope expansion is not interpreted as a failure of discipline or planning. Instead, it is analyzed as evidence that Scope boundaries were not enforced through governance mechanisms. Under REPAC rules, the absence of stabilized scope boundaries creates ambiguity about what constitutes valid work, thereby destabilizing Work and Value assessment.

Scope failure is identified analytically when scope decisions cannot be traced to authoritative constraint mechanisms.

## Governance

Decision authority was distributed informally. Escalation channels were unclear. Trade-off decisions were deferred rather than resolved. Governance mechanisms nominally existed but were not consistently exercised.

Analytically, Governance constructs were present but weakly instantiated. Authority and accountability were insufficiently constrained to regulate scope and work decisions.

REPAC treats Governance as the Superset responsible for enforcing decision authority, defining escalation channels, and resolving constraints. The rule is that Governance must be observable using consistent decision-making behaviour rather than only through documented structures.

In the walkthrough, governance roles nominally existed, but decision authority was inconsistently exercised. Under REPAC rules, governance is considered weakly instantiated when decisions are deferred, informally negotiated, or repeatedly revisited without resolution.

The ontology does not evaluate governance effectiveness in normative terms. It evaluates whether governance mechanisms were sufficient to constrain Scope, regulate Work, and arbitrate trade-offs. In this case, they were not.

## Work

Delivery teams were staffed and active. Work artifacts were produced. However, work sequencing was frequently redefined, and rework was typical due to shifting expectations.

The REPAC analysis does not directly evaluate work quality at this stage. Instead, it observes that Work activity existed but was destabilized by upstream analytical weaknesses.

REPAC applies a strict separation between Work activity and Work sufficiency. The rule is that the existence of work artifacts or effort does not imply analytical adequacy.

In the walkthrough, Work was present and continuous. REPAC analysis does not attribute failure to lack of effort or capability. Instead, it evaluates whether Work could be stabilized given upstream ambiguity in Purpose, Scope, and Governance.

Under REPAC rules, Work instability is interpreted as a downstream symptom rather than a primary analytical failure.

## Value

Value was assumed rather than specified. Metrics focused on activity completion rather than outcome realization. No agreed-upon criteria existed for determining whether delivered artifacts constituted value.

From an ontological perspective, Value constructs were implicit and unmodeled. This prevented meaningful evaluation of progress or success.

REPAC requires that Value be explicitly modeled as an evaluative construct rather than assumed as an outcome of activity. The applied rule is that Value must be definable independently of Work completion.

In the walkthrough, value was inferred from progress metrics and artifact delivery rather than from agreed evaluative criteria. REPAC identifies this as an analytical gap because Value could not be used to judge success or failure in a disciplined way.

The ontology does not ask whether value existed in practice. It asks whether Value could be logically determined. In this case, it could not.

## Communication

Information circulated frequently but inconsistently. Different stakeholder groups held incompatible interpretations of objectives, priorities, and constraints.

REPAC distinguishes communication volume from communication coherence. In this case, the communication activity existed but did not enforce shared analytical understanding.

REPAC distinguishes communication cadence from analytical coherence. The applied rule is that Communication must support shared interpretation of Purpose, Scope, Governance, and Value, not simply information exchange.

In the walkthrough, communication activity was high, but interpretations diverged across stakeholder groups. Under REPAC rules, this indicates that communication did not function as an alignment mechanism.

Communication is evaluated analytically by its effect on common understanding, not by volume or transparency.

## Collaboration

Cross-team collaboration was mandated but not structured. Teams interacted but lacked shared decision models or role clarity. Collaboration relied on goodwill rather than defined mechanisms.

Analytically, Collaboration existed as interaction without alignment.

REPAC applies the rule that Collaboration must be structurally enabled rather than socially assumed. Collaboration is evaluated based on whether roles, decision rights, and coordination mechanisms are analytically defined.

In the walkthrough, collaboration occurred through interaction rather than using structured alignment. REPAC identifies this as insufficient because collaboration could not compensate for unresolved governance and scope ambiguity.

The ontology treats collaboration failure as a structural issue rather than an interpersonal one.

## Analytical Synthesis

REPAC analysis exposes that no single domain failure explains the outcome. Instead, the failure emerged from cumulative analytical insufficiencies across Purpose, Scope, Governance, and Value. Work activity and Communication masked these deficiencies temporarily but could not compensate for them.

The initiative did not fail because individuals performed poorly. It failed because essential analytical domains were insufficiently defined, constrained, and aligned.

REPAC rules prohibit collapsing analysis into a single failing domain. The ontology requires examination of how insufficiencies accumulate across Supersets.

In this walkthrough, Purpose under-specification weakened Scope control. Weak Scope destabilized Governance enforcement. Governance ambiguity propagated instability into Work and prevented measurable Value realization. Communication and Collaboration activity masked these issues without resolving them. Causal relationships create an almost incalculable set of interactions. But it is not calculational in the numeric sense. Mathematically, REPAC gestures are structural and combinatorial, not arithmetic. REPAC is closest to structural combinatorics under constraint, not equations.

- REPAC does not compute outcomes
- REPAC enumerates the space of possible failure states created by misalignment across Supersets
- This is why single-cause explanations fail. The system state is not linear.

In this scenario, the failure is understood as structural rather than causal or behavioural.

## Analytical Complexity and Constraint Interaction

REPAC explicitly prohibits collapsing analysis into a single failing domain. Organizational outcomes are not modeled as the result of linear causation or isolated deficiencies. Instead, the ontology requires examination of how insufficiencies accumulate and interact across analytically necessary Supersets.

Each Superset is treated as necessary but not sufficient. No single domain can explain success or failure independently, and no domain can substitute for another. Failure emerges from interaction, not from isolated states.

### Supersets as Analytical Variables

## Analytical Complexity and Constraint Interaction

REPAC explicitly prohibits collapsing analysis into a single failing domain. Organizational outcomes are not modeled as the result of linear causation or isolated deficiencies. Instead, the ontology requires examination of how insufficiencies accumulate and interact across analytically required Supersets.

Each Superset is treated as necessary but not sufficient. No single domain can explain success or failure independently, and no domain can substitute for another. Failure emerges from interaction, not from isolated states.

### Supersets as Analytical Variables

For analytical clarity, each Superset can be treated as a variable representing a distinct domain of constraint:

- P = Purpose  
- S = Scope  
- G = Governance  
- W = Work  
- V = Value  
- C₁ = Communication  
- C₂ = Collaboration  

A naïve explanatory model might assert:

Failure = bad G  

or:

Failure = bad W  

REPAC explicitly forbids this reduction. Such explanations collapse structural complexity into a single-domain attribution and obscure the conditions under which failure actually emerges.

Instead, REPAC assumes that:

- Each Superset is needed but not sufficient
- Failure emerges from interactions among Supersets, not from isolated deficiencies

Formally, the analytical relationship is closer to:

Failure ∈ f(P × S × G × W × V × C₁ × C₂)

The symbol "×" does not indicate numeric multiplication. It represents a Cartesian product of analytical states. Each Superset contributes a set of possible conditions, and failure emerges from specific combinations within that multidimensional space.

### Combinatorial State Space

Each Superset can exist in more than two qualitative states. Even under conservative assumptions:

- Purpose: {clear, ambiguous, conflicting}  
- Scope: {bounded, drifting, unstable}  
- Governance: {enforced, weak, absent}  
- Work: {stable, reworked, chaotic}  
- Value: {defined, assumed, unmeasurable}  
- Communication: {aligned, noisy, divergent}  
- Collaboration: {structured, informal, fragmented}  

This yields:

3⁷ = 2,187 possible analytical configurations

This figure reflects only static combinations. It does not account for:

- Temporal change
- Feedback loops
- Cross-Superset reinforcement effects

This is why the analytical space becomes effectively incalculable in practice. The intuition that organizational failure resists simple explanation is therefore structurally justified.

### Why REPAC Does Not Attempt Numeric Calculation

REPAC intentionally stops short of numeric computation for three reasons.

First, the analytical state space is too large to compute meaningfully without arbitrary simplification.  
Second, assigning weights or probabilities would introduce false precision and imply determinism where none exists.  
Third, organizations are not closed systems. Their boundaries, constraints, and interpretations evolve over time.

Instead of computing outcomes, REPAC constrains reasoning.

The ontology enforces analytical completeness by requiring that all required domains be examined and that their interactions be made explicit.

### Formulaic Structure Without Arithmetic

REPAC may be described as formulaic in a structural sense rather than a mathematical one.

The ontology enforces the following analytical rules:

- All Supersets must be considered
- No Superset may be ignored
- No Superset may substitute for another
- Interactions must be examined explicitly rather than assumed away

This structure resembles formal systems such as type systems, constraint satisfaction frameworks, or safety proofs. Its rigor lies not in calculation, but in what forms of explanation are disallowed.

Organizational failure is not attributable to a single deficient domain. Under REPAC, failure emerges from the interaction of multiple analytically necessary domains whose misalignment produces a combinatorial space of structurally unstable states.

This framing is mathematically honest without trying to compute outcomes.

### Implications for the Failure Walkthrough

Within the worked failure walkthrough, this analytical discipline explains why no single failing domain is identified as the cause of the outcome. The failure is not attributed to governance, communication, or execution in isolation.

Instead, the walkthrough demonstrates how under-specified Purpose weakened Scope constraints, unstable Scope undermined Governance enforcement, weak Governance destabilized Work, and the absence of explicit Value criteria prevented evaluative closure. Communication and Collaboration activity masked these deficiencies without resolving them.

The outcome is therefore explained as a structurally emergent failure rather than a causally linear one.

### Analytical Integrity

REPAC preserves analytical integrity by resisting narrative convenience and post-hoc rationalization. It does not simplify complexity by ignoring it. It simplifies analysis by enforcing disciplined consideration of all analytically necessary domains and their interactions.

In this sense, REPAC does not compute answers. It prevents invalid explanations.

## Why This Is Not Root Cause Analysis

REPAC does not seek a singular root cause. The ontology demonstrates that complex organizational failure emerges from structural misalignment rather than linear causation. Attempting to isolate a single cause would oversimplify the analytical reality.

This walkthrough illustrates how REPAC enables disciplined reasoning free from collapsing explanation into storytelling convenience.

Throughout the walkthrough, REPAC rules enforce analytical neutrality. No domain is treated as primary by default, and no retrospective narrative is privileged.

This assures that the walkthrough demonstrates ontology-guided reasoning rather than case-specific explanation.

## Analytical Outcome

The outcome of this walkthrough is not a recommendation. It is an analytical account that explains why the initiative was structurally incapable of producing its intended outcomes given the state of its analytical domains.

This distinction is central to the REPAC Ontology’s purpose.
